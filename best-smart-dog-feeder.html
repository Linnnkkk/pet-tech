Dog size matters: Large dogs need feeders with bigger portion capacities (8+ cups/meal) and larger hoppers. Small dogs can use almost any feeder. Match the feeder to your dog's daily food volume.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I feed my dog with an automatic feeder?

Most dogs need 2-3% of their body weight in food per day, split into 2 meals. A 50lb dog typically eats 1.5-2.5 cups of dry food per day. Check your specific food's calorie content and consult your vet. Start with smaller portions and adjust — automatic feeders make it easy to fine-tune over a few days.

Can automatic feeders handle large dog kibble?

Some can, some can't. The PetSafe Smart Feed and Feeder-Robot handle large kibble (up to 5/8 inch) without issues. Smaller feeders like the PETLIBRO work best with kibble under 1/2 inch. Always check the maximum kibble size specification before buying.

Is a slow-feed option important for dogs?

For large, deep-chested breeds (Great Danes, German Shepherds, Dobermans), slow feeding helps prevent bloat (GDV), which is a life-threatening condition. The PetSafe Smart Feed has a built-in slow-feed mode. For small dogs, it's less critical but can help with digestion and prevent vomiting.

Do smart feeders work without WiFi?

Most smart feeders continue dispensing on their stored schedule even without WiFi — you just can't change the schedule remotely. The WOPET doesn't need WiFi at all. If your internet is unreliable, choose a feeder with good offline functionality and battery backup.

Can I use an automatic feeder as my dog's only feeding method?

While automatic feeders are reliable, we recommend having a human verify feeding at least once daily — especially for puppies, senior dogs, or dogs with health conditions. Automatic feeders are excellent tools but shouldn't completely replace human oversight of your dog's eating habits.
