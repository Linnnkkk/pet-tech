How to Choose the Right Pet Camera in 2026

Pet cameras range from $25 to $300 with wildly different features. This guide cuts through the marketing jargon and helps you pick the right camera for your specific situation.

Step 1: Decide What You Actually Need

Before looking at specs, answer one question: do you just want to see your pet, or do you want to interact with them? If you just need peace of mind that your pet is okay, a $25-40 camera is perfect. If you want to toss treats, talk to your pet, and get alerts, budget $100-170. Most people overbuy — they pay for features they never use.

Step 2: Understand Video Quality

All decent pet cameras offer 1080p video, which is plenty. Don't pay extra for 4K — you're watching a pet, not producing a movie. What actually matters is night vision quality (your pet is home alone during evening hours) and HDR (if the camera faces a window, it needs to handle bright backlight). The Wyze Cam v4 has the best night vision we've tested, with color night vision instead of the standard black-and-white infrared.

Step 3: Field of View — Why 360° Matters

A camera's field of view determines how much of the room you can see. 115 degrees covers a corner. 160 degrees covers most of a room. 360-degree rotation (like the Furbo 360) lets you remotely pan the camera to see everywhere — behind furniture, under tables, in corners. If your pet moves around a lot, 360-degree rotation is worth the premium. If your pet sleeps in one spot, a fixed camera works fine.

Step 4: Treat Tossing — Fun But Not Essential

Treat tossing is the flagship feature of pet cameras like Furbo and Petcube. It's fun and pets love it, but consider: treats only work with small, round pieces (under 1/2 inch). They can jam. And you're adding calories to your pet's diet every time you use it. If your pet has weight issues or food anxiety, skip this feature. If you want it, the Petcube Bites 2 Lite ($100) has the best treat mechanism for the price.

Step 5: Subscription Costs — The Hidden Expense

Most pet cameras offer free live streaming but charge $3-8/month for video recording, AI alerts, and cloud storage. Over 3 years, that's $108-288 in subscriptions — often more than the camera itself. If you don't need video history, skip the subscription. If you do want recording, consider security cameras like the Wyze Cam v4 or TP-Link Tapo C200 that offer free local recording to microSD cards.

Step 6: Pet-Specific vs Security Camera

Security cameras ($25-40) offer better value: better video quality, local storage, no subscriptions, and multi-camera support. You lose treat tossing and pet-focused apps. For most pet owners, a $25 TP-Link Tapo C200 does 90% of what a $170 Furbo does — just without the treats. Our honest recommendation: start with a cheap security camera. If you find you really want treat tossing or pet alerts, upgrade later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a pet camera or can I use my old phone?

Old phones can work as pet cameras with apps like AlfredCamera, but the experience is poor — limited field of view, no night vision, battery drain, and no pet-specific features. A $25 Tapo C200 is a far better experience than repurposing a phone.

Is 1080p good enough for a pet camera?

Yes. 1080p is sharp enough to see your pet clearly from across a room. 4K is overkill for pet monitoring and uses significantly more bandwidth and storage. Focus on night vision quality and field of view instead of resolution.

Can pet cameras work without internet?

Most pet cameras need internet for remote viewing. Some cameras (Tapo C200, Wyze Cam) can record to local microSD cards without internet, but you can't view the feed remotely. If you need offline monitoring, local storage cameras are your best bet.

Should I buy one expensive camera or multiple cheap ones?

For most homes, 2-3 cheap cameras ($25-40 each) covering different rooms beats one expensive camera. You get full home coverage for $75-120 instead of $170 for a single premium camera. The exception: if treat tossing is important to you, you need a pet-specific camera.
