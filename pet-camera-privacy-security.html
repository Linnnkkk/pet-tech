Pet Camera Privacy & Security: What Every Owner Should Know

A pet camera is also a home surveillance camera pointing at your living space. Before installing one, understand the privacy implications and how to protect yourself.

The Privacy Reality of Pet Cameras

Every pet camera with WiFi streams video through company servers. This means the camera manufacturer's employees could theoretically access your feed, law enforcement could subpoena recordings, and hackers could intercept footage. This isn't fear-mongering — it's how internet-connected cameras work. The question is: which companies handle this responsibly?

Which Brands Have the Best Privacy Records?

Eufy (Anker) has the strongest privacy stance — all data stored locally, no cloud dependency, end-to-end encryption. Wyze has had security incidents in the past (2019 database breach, 2023 unsecured cameras) but has improved significantly. Ring (Amazon) has the most law enforcement partnerships and shares data with Amazon's ecosystem. Furbo and Petcube have clean records but smaller security teams.

How to Secure Your Pet Camera

Five steps that dramatically reduce risk: (1) Use a strong, unique password — not one you use anywhere else. (2) Enable two-factor authentication if available. (3) Keep firmware updated — install updates promptly. (4) Create a separate WiFi network for IoT devices (most modern routers support guest networks). (5) Point the camera only where you need it — not at entryways, bedrooms, or windows showing the street.

Cloud Storage vs Local Storage

Cloud storage means your recordings live on someone else's servers. The company can see them, they can be subpoenaed, and a breach exposes them. Local storage (microSD card or NAS) keeps recordings in your home — only you have access. For maximum privacy, choose cameras with local storage like the TP-Link Tapo C200 or Eufy C120. If you must use cloud storage, check the company's data retention policy.

Pet Camera Laws You Should Know

In the US, it's generally legal to record video inside your own home. Audio recording laws vary by state — some require all-party consent for audio. If your pet camera has a microphone and you have houseguests, you may need to inform them. Recording without consent in two-party consent states (California, Florida, Illinois, and others) can be illegal. Check your state's specific laws.

Physical Privacy Features to Look For

Some cameras offer privacy shutters that physically block the lens when not in use. The Ring Indoor Cam 2nd Gen has this feature. Others offer activity zones that only record in specific areas of the frame. Both are good features for reducing unnecessary recording of your private space.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can pet cameras be hacked?

Yes, any internet-connected device can be compromised. The risk is highest with default passwords, outdated firmware, and cheap no-name brands. Using strong passwords, enabling 2FA, and keeping firmware updated reduces risk significantly. Established brands (Furbo, Petcube, TP-Link) have better security practices than unknown brands.

Is my pet camera recording all the time?

It depends on settings. Without a subscription, most cameras only stream live when you open the app. With cloud recording subscriptions, they record continuously or when motion is detected. With local microSD storage, they record continuously to the card. Check your camera's recording settings to understand what's being captured.

Should I be worried about Amazon owning Ring?

Ring has more law enforcement data-sharing partnerships than any other camera brand. Amazon also uses Ring data for product recommendations. If this concerns you, consider non-Amazon alternatives like Eufy, TP-Link, or Wyze. If you're already in the Amazon ecosystem, Ring's convenience may outweigh the privacy trade-offs.

Can I use a pet camera in an apartment?

Yes, but be aware of what your camera captures. Don't point it at shared hallways, neighbors' windows, or common areas. In rental properties, check your lease for any camera-related restrictions. Most leases don't prohibit cameras inside your unit, but shared area recording may be restricted.
